# Competition, History and Odds of Success: Faceless/Animated Long-Form Kids and Family YouTube Channels (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: Research was done with web search tools (Perplexity-grounded search and WebSearch). Direct fetches of fortune.com and undark.org were blocked by the session network, so some facts come from search-result summaries of those articles rather than the full text. Channel totals from trackers (Social Blade, Playboard, vidIQ, trendreport and similar) are labelled as third-party estimates. Revenue figures for private channels are almost always estimates.

## 1. History of the big kids/family channels: views, revenue, teams, time to grow

### Takeaway
The winners are a small set of studios that started 10 to 20 years ago, before the market was crowded: CoComelon (2006), Super Simple Songs (2006), Little Baby Bum (2011), and Pinkfong (Korean company, listed in 2025). They took years of steady output to break through, then grew into IP businesses spanning Netflix, toys and tours, often through acquisition (Moonbug). Ms Rachel, who appears on camera, shows that the fastest recent breakout came from a channel built on a real person, not a faceless one.

### Cited Findings
**CoComelon (Treasure Studio, later Moonbug)**
- The channel started in 2006 as "ThatsMEonTV" (also reported as "checkgate"), was run by Jay Jeon and his wife, and rebranded to CoComelon around 2018. A 2026-updated tracker puts it at 200M+ subscribers and 220B+ views (estimate). — [Wikipedia: CoComelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoComelon); [everything.explained.today](https://everything.explained.today/Cocomelon/)
- A company overview puts it at roughly 198M subscribers and 210B views in Oct 2025 (estimate), and reports 37.6M Netflix views for Season 8 in 2024. — [factrail.ai Moonbug overview](https://factrail.ai/organizations/moonbug-entertainment)
- Treasure Studio had about 20 employees when it was sold (Fortune, as summarized). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/cocomelon-youtube-disney-netflix-dreamworks-children-media/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoComelon)
- Moonbug bought Treasure Studio in July 2020. Candle Media (backed by Blackstone) bought Moonbug in 2021 for about $3B. The $3B is the Moonbug deal, not the CoComelon one. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoComelon); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/cocomelon-youtube-disney-netflix-dreamworks-children-media/)
- Views: about 3.5B YouTube views a month before the Moonbug deal, and about 4B a month later across the CoComelon-related channels (Fortune, as summarized). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/cocomelon-youtube-disney-netflix-dreamworks-children-media/)
- Revenue (2019 estimates only): Social Blade estimated $638K to $10.2M a month in ad revenue, and the WSJ was reported as estimating about $120M a year in ad revenue. Neither is audited. — [everything.explained.today summary](https://everything.explained.today/Cocomelon/)
- Time to grow: about 12 years from the 2006 launch to the 2018 rebrand and breakout. The 1M, 10M and 100M subscriber milestone dates could not be verified (see Gaps).

**Little Baby Bum**
- Created in 2011 by Derek and Cannis (Cat) Holder in London, and bought by Moonbug in 2018. A 2026 tracker estimates about 41.6M subscribers and 44.7B views. — [raisingstoics](https://raisingstoics.com/is-it-good-for-kids/little-baby-bum); [influtrend](https://influtrend.com/youtube/littlebabybum)

**Super Simple Songs (Skyship Entertainment, Toronto)**
- Started in 2006 by teachers working abroad. Skyship itself launched in Toronto in late 2015, co-founded by Devon Thagard and Troy McDonald. It had 10M subscribers by June 2018 and 44M+ subscribers in July 2025 (company press release). The channel marked its 20th anniversary in 2026. — [Skyship press](https://skyship.tv/press/super-simple-songs-heads-out-on-tour-across-southern-ontario/); [C21 Media](https://www.c21media.net/department/thought-leadership/skyship-keeps-it-super-simple/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Simple_Songs); [YouTube blog: 20th anniversary](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/super-simple-songs-20th-anniversary-youtube)
- Time to grow: about 12 years to reach 10M subscribers (2006 to 2018).

**Pinkfong / Baby Shark (The Pinkfong Company, Korea)**
- "Baby Shark Dance" was still YouTube's most-viewed video in 2025, with 16B+ views. — [Korea Times, Nov 2025](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/companies/20251103/company-behind-baby-shark-hopes-to-accelerate-global-growth-after-ipo-ceo); [eNCA](https://www.enca.com/business/shares-baby-shark-studio-jump-market-debut)
- Across all its channels the company reported 280M combined subscribers and 180B cumulative YouTube views (2025 filing, as reported). — [Asia Economy, Sept 2025](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025092219271528485)
- 2024 results: KRW 97.4B consolidated revenue and KRW 18.8B operating profit; content was 68% of revenue, but the YouTube share was not broken out. For the 2025 IPO the price range was KRW 32,000 to 38,000 per share, implying a market cap of roughly KRW 459 to 545B. It listed on KOSDAQ on Nov 18, 2025. — [Venture Square](https://www.venturesquare.net/en/1005956/); [Global Brands Magazine](https://www.globalbrandsmagazine.com/pinkfong-makes-a-53m-global-ipo-debut/)

**Ms Rachel (on camera, for contrast)**
- Rachel Griffin Accurso started "Songs for Littles" in 2019. Subscribers (dated snapshots): about 13M in Jan 2025, and about 21.8 to 21.9M by late 2026. — [Yahoo/Business Insider](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/youtube-star-ms-rachel-built-143645823.html); [networth.com](https://networth.com/net-worth/influencers/ms-rachel-net-worth/) (low-quality aggregator)
- Netflix has licensed compilations since Jan 2025 on a non-exclusive deal of undisclosed value. Netflix-reported views: 53.4M for Season 1 in H1 2025; in H2 2025, 46.8M for Season 1 and 26.2M for Season 2. — [dollarbreak](https://www.dollarbreak.com/ms-rachel-net-worth/) (aggregator citing Netflix engagement reports)
- Forbes was reported as estimating $26M gross earnings for Mar 2025 to Mar 2026 across all revenue sources (estimate, secondhand). Team size was not found. — [networth.com](https://networth.com/net-worth/influencers/ms-rachel-net-worth/)

**Bluey and Peppa Pig (official, licensed channels; tracker snapshot, Nov 2026, estimates)**
- Bluey official channel: about 15M subscribers and 12B views. Peppa Pig Official Channel: about 45M subscribers and 36.4B views. — [trendreport.com](https://trendreport.com/channels/stats/country/united-kingdom/kids-and-animation/)

### Inferences
- Every faceless winner above either started before 2012, when there was little competition and the "Made for Kids" ad limits did not yet exist, or is backed by an existing TV IP (Bluey, Peppa). None is a recent solo faceless launch.
- Today's winners run as studios: about 20 staff at CoComelon when it sold, while Pinkfong and Moonbug are corporations. They make money from licensing, Netflix, toys and live tours, not just AdSense. A newcomer's realistic ceiling is a very different business.
- The fastest recent breakout, Ms Rachel (2019 to about 22M subscribers), is built on a real person. Parents' trust in a human face may be an advantage that faceless channels lack.

### Gaps
- Exact dates when CoComelon passed 1M, 10M and 100M subscribers were not verified.
- No audited YouTube revenue for CoComelon, Moonbug, Super Simple Songs or Ms Rachel. All figures are estimates or company totals.
- ChuChu TV and BabyBus figures were not found in usable sources.
- Bluey licensing and compilation strategy details were not verified.

## 2. Is kids content still growing on YouTube (2024-2026)? TV/CTV viewing and the long-form compilation trend

### Takeaway
YouTube's share of US TV-screen viewing kept rising through 2025 and 2026. That favors long, lean-back content such as kids compilations played on the living-room TV. However, no reliable public figure breaks out kids content's share of watch time or of CTV viewing.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube's share of US TV viewing (Nielsen Gauge, via secondary sources): 13.1% in Aug 2025, 13.8% in June 2026, and a reported record of 15.8% in Aug 2026. These come from aggregator blogs and need checking against Nielsen directly. — [voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/youtube-tv-screen-viewing-share-statistics-2026/); [marketintelligencetools](https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/youtube-statistics/)
- YouTube said in 2024 that people watched more than 1B hours a day of YouTube on TVs worldwide, and that connected-TV views had grown 130%+ over the prior three years (as reported). — [voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/youtube-tv-screen-viewing-share-statistics-2026/)
- Nielsen research (not kids-specific) says 47% of linear and connected-TV viewing happens with someone else present. — [iaqaba](https://iaqaba.com/youtube-co-views-tv-public-view-counts/)
- Historical baseline (Pew, 2019): among channels with 250K+ subscribers, about 4% of English-language videos appeared aimed at children and averaged 153,227 views, against 99,713 for other videos. The 2% of videos featuring a child under 13 averaged nearly 3x the views. — [Pew Research 2019 PDF](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/internet/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2019/07/DL_2019.07.25_YouTube-Channels_FINAL.pdf); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2019-07-25-pew-youtube-videos-with-kids-or-video-games-get-most-views.html)
- Netflix demand for YouTube-born kids IP is still strong. CoComelon Season 8 had 37.6M views in 2024, and Ms Rachel Season 1 had 53.4M views in H1 2025. — [factrail](https://factrail.ai/organizations/moonbug-entertainment); [dollarbreak](https://www.dollarbreak.com/ms-rachel-net-worth/)

### Inferences
- The shift to TV screens suits 1 to 3 hour compilations, which parents leave playing. The large channels (CoComelon, Super Simple, Ms Rachel) all publish long compilations, and their Netflix "seasons" are the same compilations repackaged. That much is observable, but no published dataset quantifies the trend.
- Demand for kids viewing is not the problem. The question is how much of it goes to new entrants (see section 3).

### Gaps
- No Nielsen, YouTube or Comscore figure was found for kids content's share of YouTube watch time or of CTV viewing in 2024-2026.
- No dataset measures the rise of 1 to 3 hour compilations. This rests on observation only.
- Current Made-for-Kids CPM and RPM levels were not sourced. A figure of $1 to 3 RPM is often repeated but was not verified.

## 3. Saturation: the flood of AI-made faceless kids videos (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
From 2024 to 2026, major outlets (Wired, Bloomberg, NYT, Fortune, Undark) documented a flood of cheap AI-generated kids videos, often labelled "educational." The backlash brought YouTube enforcement against named channels, a stricter "inauthentic content" monetization rule in July 2025, and a coalition of 200+ groups demanding a ban on YouTube Kids. AI-made kids content is now both crowded and under policy and reputational pressure.

### Cited Findings
**Reporting timeline**
- **Wired, Mar 12, 2024:** Wired identified apparently AI-assisted kids channels by spotting new accounts posting many videos. Reality Defender rated one video ("Caring for Injured Baby Dino") 98% likely to have an AI-written script. — [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/your-kid-may-be-watching-ai-generated-videos-on-youtube/)
- **Wired (2025), covered by Tubefilter, May 2, 2025:** Wired found dozens of channels showing AI-generated cartoon gore and fetish content (cats, Minions; for example "Go Cat"), made with tools such as Midjourney. After Wired asked, YouTube terminated 2 channels and suspended monetization on 3. Tubefilter framed this as a possible "AI Elsagate." — [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/dozens-of-youtube-channels-are-showing-ai-generated-cartoon-gore-and-fetish-content/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/05/02/ai-elsagate-videos-minion-go-cat-child-safety-content/)
- **The Guardian, Aug 2025:** A Guardian analysis of Playboard data found that 9 of the 100 fastest-growing YouTube channels in July 2025 showed purely AI-generated content. This covered all channels, not only kids. — [The Guardian via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/ja/stories/newspaper/The-Guardian/AICREATED-VIDEOS-ARE-FLOODING-YOUTUBE-EXPERTS-SAY)
- **Kapwing, Nov 2025:** On a fresh account, 104 of the first 500 recommended Shorts (about 21%) were AI slop and 33% were "brainrot." Across the top 100 channels per country, Kapwing found 278 channels that were entirely AI slop, with 63B views, 221M subscribers and an estimated $117M a year in revenue (estimate). Examples: Bandar Apna Dost (India), 2.07B views, about $4.25M a year (estimate); Cuentos Fascinantes (Spanish-language, US), 5.95M subscribers, about $2.66M a year (estimate). — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/ai-slop/); [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/youtube-ai-slop-study)
- **Bloomberg, Dec 3, 2025 ("YouTube Creators Find a New Consumer for AI Slop: Babies"):** Bloomberg profiled creator and coach Monique Hinton (1M+ followers), whose workflow is ChatGPT for simple, repetitive song lyrics plus an AI video generator. She said it can earn "hundreds of dollars a day" and that AI does about 95% of the work. These are her claims, not verified. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-03/ai-slop-youtube-videos-for-kids-pretend-to-be-educational)
- **NYT, Feb 26, 2026 ("How A.I.-Generated Videos Are Distorting Your Child's YouTube Feed"):** The Times reviewed 1,000+ videos recommended to young children. It found AI videos with warped faces, extra limbs (for example, a horse hatching from an egg in an AI "Old MacDonald") and misinformation, many marketed as educational. YouTube's AI disclosure rule does not cover animated content. After the Times shared five channels, YouTube suspended all five from YPP, removed videos from YouTube Kids, and later terminated 6 channels for terms-of-service violations and 1 for spam. YouTube said it is developing AI labels for YouTube Kids. — [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/02/26/us/ai-videos-children-youtube.html); [Texarkana Gazette reprint](https://www.texarkanagazette.com/news/2026/mar/19/how-ai-generated-videos-are-distorting-your); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-strikes-down-channels-after-investigation-into-bizarre-ai-content-for-kids-3326231/)
- **Undark, Mar 20, 2026:** A feature on AI slop aimed at children. Full text was not retrieved. — [Undark](https://undark.org/2026/03/20/ai-slop-children/)
- **Fortune, Apr 1, 2026:** 200+ organizations and experts (135+ organizations) asked YouTube to label all AI content, ban AI content from YouTube Kids, and prohibit AI "made for kids" content on main YouTube. Fairplay estimated that top child-targeted AI-slop channels earn more than $4.25M a year (estimate). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/01/ai-slop-200-organizations-letter-youtube-google/); [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/sci-tech/article/advocacy-groups-urge-youtube-to-protect-kids-from-ai-slop-videos/)
- **Wired, Oct 15, 2026:** Wired describes kids' feeds full of incoherent, overstimulating AI videos and profiles a new platform (Maka) built as an alternative. YouTube says AI content on YouTube Kids is limited to a small set of high-quality channels. — [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/maka-streaming-platform-for-kids-fighting-against-ai-slop/)
- **Academic:** "Slop for Kids: an exploratory study of AI-generated videos for children on YouTube" (AoIR Selected Papers, 2025). Details were not retrieved. — [SPIR/AoIR](https://spir.aoir.org/ojs/index.php/spir/article/view/15158)

**Policy**
- **July 10, 2025:** YouTube renamed its YPP "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content." It said this is a clarification, not a new rule. The policy covers mass-produced or repetitive, template-like content with little variation, whatever tool was used. Its examples include narrated stories that differ only superficially and slideshows that reuse the same narration. AI-assisted channels can still monetize if they comply. — [YouTube Help community post](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251/response-to-creator-questions-about-ypp-policies-july-2025?hl=en)
- **Elsagate precedent (2017):** YouTube removed 150K+ videos, pulled ads from about 2M videos, and acted on 50K+ channels posing as family-friendly. Early Elsagate channels (for example "Webs and Tiaras") had more than 1B views in 2016-17. — [Wikipedia: Elsagate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsagate); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/youtube-elsagate-kids-videos-like-comment-subscribe-1849501198)
- **2019 FTC/COPPA settlement:** $170M. YouTube then barred personalized ads on "Made for Kids" content, which lowered monetization for kids channels. No reliable CPM-drop percentage was found. — [lenspov (secondary)](https://lenspov.com/research/youtube-cpm-by-niche-2026/)

### Inferences
- There is a clear policy cycle: a content flood, press exposure, then enforcement, as with Elsagate in 2017-18 and AI slop in 2025-26. A newcomer making AI-generated kids content now faces a higher risk of demonetization and possible labelling or YouTube Kids exclusion, on top of the competition.
- Large fully AI channels do exist and earn millions (Kapwing estimates). This shows the model can work at the top, but it is the same survivorship-biased top tail.
- Being "faceless" is not the risk in itself. The risk is template-like repetition, which is exactly what the "inauthentic content" policy targets.

### Gaps
- No 404 Media article on AI kids channels was found.
- No Guardian article specifically on AI kids videos was found; only the general Playboard analysis.
- No vidIQ or Playboard dataset on how many AI kids channels exist or how fast they are growing.
- No reports were verified of a Neal Mohan 2026 letter or a January 2026 policy change specific to Made-for-Kids AI content.
- The names of the five channels the NYT flagged were not retrieved.

## 4. Case studies: small faceless kids/mom channels that succeeded or failed (including demonetizations)

### Takeaway
Well-documented case studies of small faceless kids channels are rare. The verifiable examples are mostly failures under enforcement: the channels named by the NYT and Wired lost monetization or were terminated. The documented "successes" are either very large AI channels (Kapwing estimates) or self-reported claims from course sellers or coaches.

### Cited Findings
- NYT (Feb 2026): YouTube suspended the 5 channels the Times flagged from YPP, and later terminated 6 for terms-of-service violations and 1 for spam. This is a documented failure mode for AI "educational" toddler channels. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-strikes-down-channels-after-investigation-into-bizarre-ai-content-for-kids-3326231/); [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/02/26/us/ai-videos-children-youtube.html)
- Wired (2025): AI cartoon-violence channels (for example "Go Cat"): 2 terminated and 3 demonetized after Wired's inquiry. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/05/02/ai-elsagate-videos-minion-go-cat-child-safety-content/)
- Bloomberg (Dec 2025): Monique Hinton claims "hundreds of dollars a day" from AI kids songs. This is self-reported, and she also sells instruction. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-03/ai-slop-youtube-videos-for-kids-pretend-to-be-educational)
- Kapwing (Nov 2025), on the success side: Cuentos Fascinantes, 5.95M subscribers, about $2.66M a year (estimate). Bandar Apna Dost: about $4.25M a year (estimate). — [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/youtube-ai-slop-study)
- Earlier era: Elsagate channels such as "Webs and Tiaras" passed 1B views before the 2017 purge. — [Southwest Shadow (student paper)](https://southwestshadow.com/opinion/columnists/the-history-and-remergence-of-elsagate/)

### Inferences
- Success stories that circulate online (course sellers, "faceless channel" YouTubers) are mostly self-reported and come from people who profit from selling the method. Treat them as marketing.
- The failure pattern is consistent: high-volume, low-variation, AI-animated "educational" content gets flagged under inauthentic content, spam or child safety. This happens especially after press attention.

### Gaps
- No verified, sourced case study was found of a small (under 100K subscribers) faceless kids or mom channel with disclosed earnings, either successful or failed.
- No reliable collection of Reddit r/PartneredYoutube reports of "reused/inauthentic content" rejections specific to kids channels could be verified.
- No Business Insider profile of a small faceless kids channel was found.

## 5. Survivorship bias: share of channels that reach YPP, and median earnings

### Takeaway
YouTube views are extremely concentrated. Bärtl's 2018 study found the top 3% of channels got about 85% of views, and 96.5% of creators earned below the US poverty line from ads (estimated). YouTube has never disclosed what share of channels reach YPP. 3M+ channels are in YPP, out of a total base of channels well above that, so most never qualify.

### Cited Findings
- Bärtl (2018, Convergence journal): the top 3% of channels got about 85% of views over the period studied, and nearly 90% in 2016. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1354856517736979); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/73485-top-three-percent-youtubers-account-90-percent-all.html)
- Bärtl (2018): 96.5% of creators would earn less than the US poverty line from ad revenue, assuming $1 RPM (a historical estimate). — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/40537244/96-5-of-youtube-creators-dont-make-above-the-u-s-poverty-line); [Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/startups-tech/technology/success-youtube-still-means-life-poverty-study)
- YPP has more than 3M channels (YouTube statement). That is a membership count, not a share of all channels. — [Perplexity summary of YouTube statements; no primary URL retrieved; see Gaps]
- The full-monetization threshold is 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 valid public watch hours in the past 12 months. For Made-for-Kids channels, personalized ads have been disabled since the 2019 COPPA settlement ($170M). — [lenspov](https://lenspov.com/research/youtube-cpm-by-niche-2026/)

### Inferences
- Even before AI, the expected result for a random new channel was never reaching YPP. Kids content then adds lower RPMs (no personalized ads, no comments or notifications on Made-for-Kids content), competition from studios with 10+ years of back catalog, and active enforcement against templated AI output.
- A realistic newcomer plan should budget for a long build (the winners took about 12 years) or a differentiated, high-effort product. Studio-quality, original IP is the only kind of faceless kids content still defended by policy and favored by parents.

### Gaps
- There is no official or credible published figure for the percentage of YouTube channels that reach YPP or 1,000 subscribers. Widely shared numbers in blogs could not be sourced.
- There is no reliable median YouTube creator earnings figure for 2023-2026 (vidIQ, Linktree and Goldman Sachs figures were not retrieved).
- There is no data on success rates specifically for faceless or "cash-cow" channels. The only evidence available is anecdotal and self-reported.
