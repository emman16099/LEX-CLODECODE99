## 3. Editing style — what they did

1. **Starts and ends on the same shot.** Second 0 and second 62 are the same finished kitchen. When the video loops, it feels seamless, so people watch twice.
2. **The hook is a question over the result.** You see the payoff first, then "why?" makes you stay for the answer.
3. **Fastest at the start.** About 0.7 s per shot in the first 7 seconds, then about 1.2 s.
4. **Steps go in order.** Base → level → toe kick → boxes → clamps → wall fixing → drawers → fronts → reveal. Easy to follow.
5. **Wide → close → close → wide rhythm.** A wide shot every few seconds shows where we are. Close-ups show the skill (level bubble, screw, glue dot, spacer card).
6. **Satisfying "proof" close-ups.** Level bubble centered (5 s), clamps pulling edges flush (29 s), spacer cards making even gaps (52–55 s). These are the money shots.
7. **Phone footage, natural light, one room.** No fancy camera, no graphics, no zooms. Just straight cuts.
8. **No captions.** It only works with sound on. Lexie Wood can beat this.

## 4. The voice — what they did

- **A third-person narrator ("he…"), not the builder.** The builder never talks on camera (he's wearing earbuds). This is the "explainer channel over someone's footage" format.
- **Fast and nonstop:** about 190 words per minute, gaps under half a second. It feels urgent and keeps you watching.
- **Lively pitch** that goes up and down. It doesn't sound bored.
- **Music sits very low** under the voice, or there's only room sound.
- **Weak spot:** sometimes the words don't match the picture. At 24–28 s the voice talks about "drawer slides… to the wall" while we see boxes and clamps. One line also doesn't quite make sense: drawer slides don't secure cabinets to the wall.

## 5. Full transcript (original, English)

> Why do these kitchen cabinets fit so perfectly? First, he installs a plywood base to create a strong foundation, then checks that everything is level. Next, he marks the uneven spots on the floor and trims the toe kick to match the room. Once it fits, he secures it from behind, hiding every fastener. Now the cabinet boxes go onto the base and quick clamps pull them tightly together, keeping the fronts aligned with no visible gaps. Then he installs heavy-duty drawer slides inside each cabinet to secure everything to the wall. He finds the studs behind the drywall and attaches a reinforcement board along the top, giving the cabinets a solid fixing point. Finally, he slides in the solid wood drawers and positions the white decorative fronts. Small dots of hot glue hold each front temporarily while he makes tiny adjustments. Then he screws them permanently from the inside, and that's what makes the finish look so clean. Every cabinet lines up, every drawer sits straight, and the whole kitchen looks custom built, even though the boxes are prefabricated, a smart installation from start to finish with each detail checked before the reveal.

## 6. Lexie Wood Taglish version

**Rules followed:** new words, not the original's lines (we copy the *method*, not the sentences). Brand spelled Lexie Wood. Grade 5 words.
**Buyer:** homeowner / condo owner, problem aware. **Spine:** 3P (Problem → Pursuit → Payoff).
**Must shoot your own footage.** Don't reuse this video's clips; that's someone else's work.

### Hook options (all OUR WORDS - test)
- **A (recommended):** "Hindi pantay ang sahig. Pero pantay ang cabinets. Paano?"
  *Why:* pain first, plus two things that don't usually go together (crooked floor, straight cabinets). Most homes have this problem.
- **B:** "Tingnan mo 'yung gilid. Walang siwang. Paano 'yun?"
- **C:** "Bakit 'yung ibang kitchen, tagilid ang pinto after one year?"
