# Kallaway-Style Teaching Script

## Purpose
Write a short vertical teaching video where one person talks to camera and teaches a **named method in 3 steps**. The style comes from a Kallaway Marketing short ("The Triple Hook Method", 2:14, ~494 spoken words). See `references/source-breakdown.md` for the annotated teardown.

Copy the **structure and moves**, never the creator's words, names, or claims. The output is the user's own method in the user's own voice.

## Inputs (ask only for what is missing)
1. **Topic / method** the video teaches.
2. **Audience** — who watches and what they want.
3. **Real proof** the user can stand behind: numbers, results, experience, examples. If none, write without stats.
4. **CTA goal** — comment keyword for a DM, follow, link, or none.
5. **Length** — default 60–90 s. The source runs ~2:14 at ~220 words per minute (fast).

If the user has a brand profile or voice file, load it first and match its voice over this style.

## The structure (6 beats)

| Beat | Time share | Job | Source pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | ~8% | Topic + big payoff + name the method | "Here's how to [do X] so [extreme result]. This is called the [Name] method. And if you do this right, [vivid outcome]." |
| 2. Step 1 | ~25% | The basic step | "Step one is [name]. This is the basic step, but so many people still get this wrong." |
| 3. Step 2 | ~30% | The step people miss | "Step two is where most people mess up. This is called [name]." |
| 4. Step 3 | ~15% | The advanced "secret" step | "Step three is really where [the payoff] is. [Few people] know how to do this. I call it [name]." |
| 5. Recap | ~7% | All 3 steps in one sentence | "That combination of [step 1 verb], [step 2 verb], and then [step 3 verb] is the [name] formula." |
| 6. CTA | ~10% | Bridge to the deeper resource | "If you want to learn how to actually do this with examples, [resource]. Comment '[keyword]' and I'll send it to you." |

### Inside each step (use this order)
1. **Name it** — give the step a short, sticky label ("the context", "the lean", "the snapback").
2. **Why it matters** — one line about how viewers or customers actually behave.
3. **The rule** — one "must" sentence ("The first line must…").
4. **Ways to do it** — a list of 3–4 options in one breath ("You can say it, show it, or write it — ideally all three").
5. **Check** (at least once in the video) — a yes/no question the viewer can test their own work with.

### Escalation (the retention engine)
Each step must sound **more important than the last**: basic → commonly missed → rare/secret. This keeps people watching for the "best" step. The last step should be genuinely the most valuable idea, or the escalation is a broken promise.

### One analogy
Put a single concrete analogy at the hardest step (source: fishing — hook it swimming one way, yank the line the other). Pick an everyday image the audience already knows. One only.

## Sentence style
- Spoken, not written: contractions, "you", "gotta/wanna" only if the user talks that way.
- Short sentences. Most under 15 words. One idea each.
- Turn-starters to move between beats: "Alright,", "Now,", "So,".
- Coin 3–5 named terms and repeat them exactly. Naming makes ideas feel like a system.
- Lists of three ("say it, show it, write it").
- Direct rules with "must" and "the goal is".
- No filler intro ("Hey guys, today…"). The first word starts the hook.

## Visual layer (write it beside the script)
- Captions word by word, centered.
- A **title card** every time a named term appears (e.g. "Step 2: The Lean").
- A simple diagram or example clip for each step; the speaker shrinks to a small inset box when a graphic is on screen.
- A list card at the recap showing all 3 steps.

## Honesty rules (do not skip)
The source leans on claims it never proves: "scientifically impossible to skip", "we have data", "like 1% of people", "works every single time". Do **not** copy these.
- Use a stat only if the user supplied it and can show its source.
- Replace absolutes with true, specific language: "this kept more viewers past 3 seconds in our last 10 videos" beats "works every time".
- Escalation words ("most people miss this") must be believable for the audience.
- In paid ads, unproven claims can break platform ad rules and consumer law. Flag any claim that needs proof.
- Never present the result as the original creator's work or use their method names.

## Output format
1. **Title / named method**
2. **Script table**: Time | Spoken words | On-screen text / visual
3. **Word count and estimated length** (at ~150 wpm for normal pace; ~220 wpm for the source's fast pace)
4. **Claims to verify** — every number or strong claim, with what proof is needed
5. **2 alternate hooks**

## Self-check before handing over
- Does the first line name the topic within the first 3–5 words?
- Is there a named method and 3 named steps?
- Does each step rise in importance, and is step 3 really the best?
- Is there exactly one analogy?
- Does the recap fit in one sentence?
- Is every stat real and sourced, with no "every time / impossible / 1%" hype?
- Would it sound natural read out loud in under the target time?
