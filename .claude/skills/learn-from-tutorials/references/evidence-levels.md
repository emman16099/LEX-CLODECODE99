# Evidence levels (how much should the agent trust a lesson?)

| Level | Name | What counts | What the agent does with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Tested by us | We tried it on our own posts/ads and the numbers moved (and we compared against a version without it) | Rulebook → "Proven for us". Use by default. |
| **B** | Seen in many winners | The pattern shows up in 5+ over-performing videos from 3+ different creators in the last 12 months | Rulebook → "Strong bets", and test queue. Use it, but test it. |
| **C** | One expert, with a reason | A credible creator explains it with an example or a number we can check | Test queue. Can try it, can't rely on it. |
| **D** | Opinion or pitch | No example, no number, "trust me", or it is the bait for a course / DM funnel | Keep the card, never promote. |

## Things that lower the grade

- The tip is the hook of a "comment X and I'll send you…" video (the video's
  goal is comments, not teaching).
- The creator sells a product about exactly this tip.
- Numbers with no source ("this gets 3x more views").
- About an app version, algorithm or feature older than ~12 months.
- Only shown in a different niche (a dance trend may not work for kitchens).

## Things that raise the grade

- Several creators who don't know each other say the same thing.
- The creator shows their own before/after numbers.
- We see it inside over-performing videos, not just in a tutorial.
- It matches how people's attention works in general (curiosity, clear
  promise, surprise, story), not only one platform's current rules.

## Survivorship warning

Looking only at winners can fool you: losers may have used the same trick.
When possible, also look at average or low performers from the same creator.
If the trick is everywhere, it is "normal", not the reason for winning.
