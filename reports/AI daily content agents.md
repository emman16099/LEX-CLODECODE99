# Many AIs Pick Posts; Almost None Read Email

Yes. Many companies already sell an AI that tells you what to post. As of November 2026, the closest match to your idea is [Vamos](https://getvamos.ai/). It sends you "Today's post is ready" as a script every day at 07:00. It also studies your own posts and learns from how they did. Other close matches are [Holo](https://tryholo.ai/), [Emelyn](https://emelyn.ai/), [Native](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Vdd-native-artificial-intuition-for-marketing), [Pattern](https://www.producthunt.com/products/pattern-io?launch=pattern-6dc2a2e6-746d-41da-85eb-cb2fbdbd4ae4) and [Naise AI](https://www.producthunt.com/products/naise-ai). For YouTube, [vidIQ Daily Ideas](https://vidiq.com/features/daily-ideas/) and [TubeBuddy Next Video Ideas](https://www.tubebuddy.com/tools/next-video-ideas) do it too. Big apps like Hootsuite, HubSpot and Sprout Social have added the same kind of helper. Some parts of your idea are now common: writing in your brand voice, ideas based on trends, drafting posts and scheduling them. Learning from post results is spreading fast, but almost all of the proof comes from the companies' own websites. Two parts are still rare. First, the researchers found no big product that reads your email inbox for post ideas. One company lists it as "not available yet," and the rest are do-it-yourself recipes. Second, no product puts all five pieces together: Google Trends, news, your inbox, a library of your own files and a learning loop. That gap is where your idea is new.

## Words to know before you read the lists

Some words come up again and again. This table explains them in plain words.

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| AI agent | A computer helper that does several steps on its own, not just one answer. |
| Brand voice | The way your posts sound, like friendly, funny or serious. |
| Knowledge library (or knowledge base) | Files you give the AI to read and use, like notes on psychology or storytelling. |
| Feedback loop (or closed loop) | The AI checks how your posts did (likes, views, comments) and uses that to pick better ideas next time. |
| Outlier | A video that did much better than that channel's usual videos. |
| Social listening | Watching what people say online about a topic or a brand. |
| Credits | Tokens you spend each time the AI does a task. |
| Template | A ready-made recipe you copy and change. |
| n8n, Make, Zapier | "Connect-the-apps" tools. You link blocks together, a bit like Lego. |
| API | A way for one app to ask another app for data. It often costs a little each time. |
| GitHub, repo, open source | GitHub is a website where people share code. A repo is one project's folder there. Open source means anyone can see and use the code for free. |
| RSS | A feed that lists new articles from a website. |
| CRM | Software that keeps your list of customers. |
| MCP | A plug that lets an AI like Claude or ChatGPT use another app. |
| SEO | Getting your website found on Google. |
| YC | Y Combinator, a famous program that funds new startups. "F26" means its Fall 2026 group. |
| Product Hunt | A website where new apps launch. |

One warning applies to every list below. Most facts come from each company's own website. That means they show what the company *says* the tool does. Nobody has tested how well these tools work. Prices are list prices seen in late November 2026. Some pages showed Black Friday sales.

## Big social media apps now suggest posts, and some learn from your results

These are the large, well-known apps that schedule posts. Every one now has some AI. But they work at very different levels. Most only write when you type a request. A smaller group comes up with topics by itself, using trends or your own results.

The strongest ones are [Hootsuite Wisdom](https://www.hootsuite.com/platform/owly-writer-ai), [HubSpot's Social Media Agent](https://www.hubspot.com/products/marketing/social-media-ai-agent), [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/ai/) and [Publer](https://publer.com/blog/publer-ai-with-brand-voices-and-analytics/). Hootsuite launched Wisdom on June 24, 2026. It says Wisdom is built on **"15+ years of proprietary social data and more than 150M monitored data sources"** ([Hootsuite press release](https://www.hootsuite.com/newsroom/press-releases/hootsuite-rebuilds-for-ai-era-introducing-wisdom)). Publer lets you upload up to 5 files and link your results. It then **"learn[s] directly from your performance for the past 30 days"** ([Publer blog](https://publer.com/blog/publer-ai-with-brand-voices-and-analytics/)). None of these apps sends a clear "post this today" message every day. The closest are Hypefury, which can refill your post queue every 6 hours, and Supergrow, which tells you what to talk about next week ([Hypefury](https://hypefury.com/llm-info); [Supergrow](https://www.supergrow.ai/individuals)).

## Creator tools already push fresh ideas every day, mostly for YouTube

This group is for video makers. YouTube tools look at "outliers" and your own channel. Only two say they send new ideas every day *and* learn from you. [vidIQ Daily Ideas](https://vidiq.com/features/daily-ideas/) gives each idea a view guess. It also changes as you save or skip ideas, and as you post new videos ([vidIQ help](https://support.vidiq.com/en/articles/7993395-personalized-and-customized-video-ideas)). [TubeBuddy Next Video Ideas](https://www.tubebuddy.com/tools/next-video-ideas) reads your videos *and your comments*, so viewer questions become ideas. You swipe to keep or skip each one. One surprise: **none of the YouTube tools name Google Trends or the news as an input**. Their "trends" come from inside YouTube only.

The general trend tools are different. Google Trends, Exploding Topics and Glimpse show what is rising. But they stop there and do not tell you what to post. [BuzzSumo](https://buzzsumo.com/) is the exception. It says it can **"Generate content ideas and briefs at the click of a button."**

## New "AI marketing employee" startups promise the full daily loop

In 2025 and 2026, a wave of startups appeared. They call themselves an "AI content strategist," "AI social media manager" or "AI CMO" (CMO means chief marketing officer, the boss of marketing). Most follow the same steps. You paste your website. The AI learns your brand. It plans, writes and posts. Then it checks your results. Almost all of them still ask you to tap "approve" before a post goes out.

The most money and growth sits with [Holo](https://tryholo.ai/). It raised a **€5 million seed round on October 15, 2026**, and says it reached **$5 million a year in sales within 252 days** ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/10/15/vilnius-based-holo-raises-eur5m-to-bring-ai-powered-marketing-to-small-businesses/)). Tech.eu is a news site, not Holo, so this is one of the few outside checks. [Native](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Vdd-native-artificial-intuition-for-marketing) explains its learning the most clearly. Its "Norn" model ranks ideas by four things: your brand personality, "what happened after content was published," trends, and which ideas you approve, rewrite or reject. Native claims over 2,000 companies use it. Still, **none of these startups uses your email inbox as a source of ideas.** Native works "right from your inbox," but only as a place to give orders ([YC: Native](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/native)).

## Nobody big reads your inbox yet; only recipes and one promise do

This is the rarest part of your idea. The researchers checked dozens of products. **No big, established product reads your email inbox to find post ideas.** The clearest sign that people want it is [LigoSocial](https://ligosocial.com/integrations/gmail). It lists a Gmail feature to "Turn newsletters and important email threads into LinkedIn post ideas," but says, **"It is not available yet."** [Supergrow](https://www.supergrow.ai/individuals) comes close in a different way: it finds ideas in your meetings and Slack chats, but not email.

Today, people who want "email in, post ideas out" build it themselves. One ready-made template stands out. [Lindy's Support Inbox Content Creator](https://www.lindy.ai/templates/support-inbox-content-creator?f61d71a4_page=2) reads your last 24 hours of customer support emails. It finds common themes and drafts LinkedIn and X posts. That is a smart idea: real customer questions become posts.

## Two of your five pieces are rare; the rest are common

Your idea has five parts. Here is how often each one already exists.

| Part of your idea | How common is it? | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| Writing in your brand voice | **Very common.** Almost every tool does it. | Publer, Metricool, Canva, Supergrow, Typefully, Emelyn, HubSpot and many more |
| Ideas from social trends | **Common.** | Hootsuite Wisdom, Sprout, Later, vidIQ, TubeBuddy, Virlo, Vamos, Native, Emelyn |
| Telling you what to post *every day* | **Getting common.** | Vamos (07:00 script), vidIQ Daily Ideas, Subscribr, Holo, Okara, many n8n and Zapier recipes |
| Learning from your post results (closed loop) | **Getting common, but mostly unproven.** About 20 tools claim it. No one has tested it. | Vamos, Native, Holo, Emelyn, Naise, Supergrow, Lately, Publer, HubSpot, vidIQ, TubeBuddy, Gumloop, AutoViralAI |
| Uploading a big library of your own files (psychology, storytelling, past posts) | **Uncommon.** Most tools only read your website or a few sample posts. | Hootsuite (PDF and Word), Publer (up to 5 files), Jasper, Copy.ai, Writesonic. The last three do not watch trends or results. |
| Google Trends as a named input | **Rare in products.** Common in DIY recipes. | n8n 7372, 4352, 3560, 13707, 21914, 15017; trendflow-ai; TrendPulse |
| News as an input | **Mixed.** | ContentStudio, BuzzSumo, Naise, Feedly; many RSS recipes |
| Reading your email inbox for ideas | **Very rare.** No big product does it. | LigoSocial (promised, not built), Lindy template, n8n 3509, Gemini Spark and Claude Code recipes |
| All five together in one product | **Not found.** | Nobody |

Two warnings matter here. First, the "learns from results" claims mean different things. Some tools only copy your *writing style* from old posts, like Typefully, Planable and Metricool. That is not the same as checking which posts got the most views. Others, like Later, learn from your *whole category*, not from your own account. Second, no company explains how its learning works. They do not say which numbers they use, how fast they learn, or whether they learn from you alone or from all their customers ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/products/marketing/social-media-ai-agent); [Publer](https://publer.com/blog/publer-ai-with-brand-voices-and-analytics/)).

The research also has gaps. Many prices were not shown, especially for the newest startups. Some niche LinkedIn tools were not checked for email features. So an inbox-reading product could exist outside what was searched.

## Conclusion

The "AI that tells you what to post" is no longer new. It is a crowded race. Big apps, YouTube tools and a fresh wave of YC-backed startups all sell some version of it. Many start at $29 to $49 a month, and some have free plans. What you cannot buy yet is the whole package in one place. That would be your inbox, Google Trends, the news and a deep library of your own files, all feeding one daily plan that gets smarter from your real results. The inbox is the clearest open door. One company has promised it but not built it, and only recipe-builders and one Lindy template use it today. That is also the most personal input, because your customers' real questions sit there.

There is also a warning sign. [Icon](https://techstartups.com/?p=131932) shut down, and [Blaze](https://www.blaze.ai/) moved from "autopilot" to "You make the calls." Both show that buyers do not fully trust an AI to post on its own. So the winning version of your idea is probably a daily *advisor* that you approve, not a robot that posts by itself. The pieces to build it already exist as free parts. You could join an n8n trend recipe, a Gmail reader and the [n8n results-review template](https://n8n.io/workflows/22323-review-social-post-performance-and-plan-content-experiments-with-sheets-and-openai/). [AutoViralAI](https://github.com/kgarbacinski/AutoViralAI) shows how the learning loop can work.
